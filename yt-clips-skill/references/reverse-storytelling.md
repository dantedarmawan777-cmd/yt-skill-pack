# Reverse-storytelling assembly

Credit: this is Briar Cochran's clipping method (@briarcochran on Instagram —
he runs short form for major podcasts; see instagram.com/p/Db13L6fBk7o). It is
the assembly spine of this skill.

1. **Start with the segment that matters most.** Out of the full video, find
   the one conversation that will hit, and isolate it (might be ~7 minutes).
2. **Inside it, find TWO sentences**: the **climax** (the punchline/payoff)
   and the **hook** (an open loop that points at the punchline). They can be
   far apart and in either order in the original — you just need both.
3. **Chop and reorder**: hook to the FRONT; climax delayed to ~60% into the
   clip (40-60 seconds in on a 60-90s clip — on a 25s Reel, 60% ≈ 15s: use
   the proportion, not the absolute number).
4. **Context bridge in the middle**: the detours, stories, and supporting
   points the speaker used to reach the main point go between hook and climax.
5. **Be ruthless — cut exactly two things**: repeated explanations (same thing
   said twice differently → keep one) and anything off the hook→climax
   through-line.
6. **End the clip once the climax hits.** Nothing after.
7. **On-screen text complements the hook** — it foreshadows the climax even
   harder than the spoken words.

His claim, which this skill's runs keep confirming: editing polish doesn't
make clips perform — this selection-and-reorder craft does.
