# Clipping frameworks research — 2026 synthesis

Researched 12/08/2026. Briar Cochran's reverse-storytelling method (see reverse-storytelling.md) is the ASSEMBLY method; this file covers SELECTION (what to clip) and SCORING (how to rank candidates).

## 1. What the big frameworks agree on

- **OpusClip virality score** (help.opus.pro/docs/article/virality-score): 4 components — Hook, Flow, Value, Trend. Scans for **self-contained moments**. Caveat: independent reviews (BIGVU, eesel) show the score is unreliable — clips rated 40 sometimes beat clips rated 85, ~40% of auto-clips get discarded. Lesson baked into this skill: **score is a ranking heuristic, not truth — always present candidates for human pick**.
- **Jenny Hoyos** (marketingexamined.com playbook): Hook (≤3s) → Foreshadow (conflict + promised payoff) → Story (but/then transitions) → Ending (peak emotion). Target 90%+ retention. Storytelling = CHANGE. "But" is the scroll-stopper word. She restructures the whole video for a stronger hook — hook quality outranks source order (agrees with Briar's reorder principle).
- **Hormozi's team**: every long-form contains **5-10 shorts**; they clip where the retention graph spikes. Opens = bold statement / contrarian take / pain question, zero intro. Cuts every 1-3s, all fillers removed, word-level captions.
- **Vizard/Riverside/Descript**: scan for high-energy moments, controversial takes, semantic/emotional shifts. Same unreliability reputation as Opus.

## 2. Clippability markers — what to flag in a transcript

Ranked roughly by how often agencies cite them:

1. **Contrarian / hot take** — bold claim against conventional wisdom. The single most-cited clippable type.
2. **Specific numbers** — real dollars, percentages, timeframes. "Real numbers outperform generic advice."
3. **Complete micro-story** — setup → turn → resolution, deliverable under ~45s. Self-contained stories beat isolated clever moments.
4. **Tactical "here's how"** — concrete steps → drives saves.
5. **List moment** — "three things nobody tells you about X."
6. **Emotional spike / energy shift** — calm→intense, serious→funny. "Listen for the reaction, not the topic."
7. **Vulnerable confession** — vulnerability outperforms polish.
8. **Bold quotable claim** — captionable as a question.
9. **Before/after transformation** — change IS the story.
10. **Myth-bust / surprising stat** — contradicts popular assumption → saves.

**Hard gates (disqualify before scoring):**
- **Self-containment test**: "if it needs the previous ten minutes to make sense, it's a highlight for fans, not a clip for strangers" (transcriptr.ai). No unresolved pronouns ("like I said earlier", "that thing we discussed").
- **Send test**: would you send this 60s to a friend saying "you need to hear this"?

**Expected yield**: 8-15 candidate moments per hour of source (klypse.app); Hormozi: 5-10 per video.

## 3. Scoring rubric (synthesis — no single published rubric exists)

| Dimension | Weight | High score looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Hook strength | 35% | First line works cold with zero context; contrarian claim, number, question, or power word; honestly relates to the clip's content (no bait) |
| Self-containment | 20% | Complete thought; a stranger gets full meaning; no dangling references |
| Payoff / completeness | 20% | Resolves the tension the hook creates; ends on the strongest beat, not a trail-off |
| Emotional charge | 15% | Surprise, humour, tension, vulnerability; energy shift WITHIN the clip |
| Value density | 10% | Every beat earns its place; specific > generic; save-worthy or argue-worthy |

Failure modes (attentioneco.com QA playbook): payoff unrelated/underwhelming vs hook = clickbait perception; hook and payoff targeting different audiences; forced transition between hook and body.

## 4. Platform lengths (2026 data — conflicts noted)

- **TikTok**: 24-38s — complete hook-value-payoff arc with high completion.
- **IG Reels**: CONFLICTING — 7-15s highest completion (60-80%) vs 15-30s best engagement (5.8%). For clipped talking-head: **target 15-30s**; sub-15s only for pure punchline clips. >45s rarely holds above 30% retention.
- **YouTube Shorts**: CONFLICTING — 30-45s retention-optimal vs 50-60s view-maximal (4.1M avg views in one dataset). Shorts tolerates the longest clips; **30-60s is the working range**.
- **LinkedIn**: 60-120s — most room for talking-head.
- **Universal**: algorithms reward COMPLETION PERCENTAGE, not seconds watched. A 20s clip at 90% beats a 60s clip at 40%. Cut to the shortest version that still delivers the payoff.
- 65% of viewers who survive the first 3s watch ≥10s; 45% watch 30s+ (Facebook research via Kapwing).

Note the tension with Briar's "delay the climax 40-60s in": on a 25s Reel, 60% depth = ~15s. **Use the 60% proportion, not the absolute 40-60s number, for short clips.** The absolute number only applies to 60-90s clips.

## 5. Hook + edit rules

- **Cold-open on the most provocative line** even if it occurs mid-answer. "Start half a second before the best word hits." Kill the warm-up — never include the rambling lead-up.
- First spoken word within **0.5s** of video start. Hook fully landed by 1.5-3s.
- **Re-ordering is standard practice** — pull the punchline/claim to position 0 and let explanation follow (Riverside, Lower Street). A clip = TWO+ timestamp spans, not one contiguous cut.
- Mid-clip: cuts every 1-3s, remove pauses/fillers, word-level captions, must work sound-off.
- **Endings**: end on the strongest beat or cliffhanger, never a trail-off. Seamless loop (end mid-momentum so the start completes the thought) works for story/humour clips ≤35s, NOT for tutorials. "End mid-energy, not mid-sentence."
